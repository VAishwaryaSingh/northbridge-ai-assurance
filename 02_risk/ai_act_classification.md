## 2. Reasoning

### 2.1 Is it an AI system?

The AI Act defines an AI system as a machine-based system that operates with some autonomy and infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, recommendations or decisions. The European Commission's guidance on the definition indicates that systems that only execute rules defined solely by people are not intended to be caught.

| Component | Nature | View |
|---|---|---|
| Reconciliation matcher, rules A1 to A6 | Fixed rules and constants written by the developer (`matcher.py`, `rules.py`) | Not AI |
| A7 median/MAD modified z-score | Classical statistics with a fixed cutoff (`statistical.py:13-60`) | Likely not AI (basic statistical estimation) |
| A7 IsolationForest option (`random_state=0`) | Unsupervised machine learning fitted on the data at run time (`statistical.py:80`), not the default | Arguably an AI component when enabled; **[uncertain]** whether it would meet the definition in practice |
| Variance explainer | String templates over SQL aggregates | Not AI |

Whether the IsolationForest path is used in any deployed configuration should be confirmed in Phase 4 (the dashboard calls the default method through `detect_all`).

### 2.2 Territorial and role considerations

Northbridge is a portfolio demonstration using fictional or vendor sandbox data, publicly viewable. The developer is UK-based. It is not placed on the EU market as a product or service, and there is no deployer processing real data. These facts support the "outside scope" conclusion but are not relied on alone: the answer to 2.1 and 2.3 stands independently.

### 2.3 Annex III (high-risk) check

The Annex III categories cover biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services (including creditworthiness and certain insurance pricing), law enforcement, migration and border control, and administration of justice and democratic processes. Bookkeeping reconciliation and anomaly flagging for accountants is not among them. If the tool were used to assess individuals' creditworthiness or to make employment decisions from ledger data, this would need reassessment.

### 2.4 Transparency (Art. 50)

Art. 50 applies to systems that interact directly with people, generate synthetic content, perform emotion recognition or biometric categorisation, or generate deepfakes. None of these apply. The dashboard's variance sentences are generated by templates and describe the ledger's own data.

### 2.5 Dates and status

The master plan includes dates for EU AI Act obligations and a reference to a "Digital Omnibus" amendment. Those have **not been verified** in this review and are not relied on for the conclusion above. Verify the current status of Articles 3, 5, 50 and Annex III (and any postponement of high-risk obligations) from the Official Journal or the Commission's site before citing dates.

## 3. UK principles assessment

The UK has no single AI statute. Regulators apply five cross-sector principles. The assessment below is against the current design and is informal.

| Principle | Assessment | Gap or note | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety, security and robustness | Partially met | Deterministic behaviour helps. Robustness on unseen data is untested; excessive QuickBooks scope; plaintext tokens; no automated tests | R01, R16, R17, R20 |
| Appropriate transparency and explainability | Partially met | Rules are inspectable and explanations are templated (good). no intended-use or limitations statement for users; hard-coded score on public app; explanations imply causation | R10, R15, R26, R27 |
| Fairness | Largely not relevant | No individuals are scored or ranked. Contact names appear in outputs. Reassess if used on personal data | R19 |
| Accountability and governance | Not met | No owner, intended use, sign-off, audit trail or change control record | R14, R20, R21, R24, R25 |
| Contestability and redress | Not met | No way to record that a flag was wrong, override it, or trace a result to a version | R14, R24 |

## 4. Other regimes noted

- **UK GDPR / ICO guidance on AI:** relevant only if real personal data is processed. Ledger contact names can be personal data for sole traders. Currently fictional or sandbox (R19).
- **Automated decision-making (UK GDPR Art. 22 as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025):** not relevant, as no decisions about individuals are made. Commencement of provisions is staged and should be checked if scope changes.
- **PRA SS1/23:** not legally applicable (not a regulated firm's model), used as a good-practice reference for inventory, validation and change control.

## 5. Triggers for reassessment

Add an LLM or ML model; enable IsolationForest in any deployed path; use with real client data; use to decide on individuals; place on the EU market as a product; any change to the AI Act's scope or dates.
